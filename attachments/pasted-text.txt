---

1. Deprecate “Prevent” → Promote “Buddy”

The VNA is no longer a fault-prevention gate that forces AMBER/RED before the demodulator sees an error. Instead, it becomes a buddy sensor:

· It does not block or prevent; it informs the other subsystems.
· Its weaknesses (calibration drift, cost, isolation requirements) are compensated by other components (Fano validator, trust score, manual inspection queue).
· Its strength (continuous, calibrated S11/TDR) is shared as a buddy attribute to the teleprompter, the defrost controller, and the Metal Runtime.

In the state machine, this means:

· The VNA’s output does not directly force a state transition.
· It contributes to the trust score and the Fano/heptadic integrity input, which then participate in the dual quadration commit.
· A VNA variance becomes a buddy signal: “Here is my weakness/strength; adjust your evaluation.”

3. Baron Plateaus as High-Efficiency Operating Regions

A Baron plateau is a stable region in the system’s parameter space where:

· the trust score is high,
· the Fano/heptadic validator passes,
· the VNA variance is within baseline,
· and the dual quadration kernel commits CYAN.

These plateaus are not points; they are regions of tolerance. The system should prefer to operate on a plateau rather than chase a single “optimal” value. The greatest efficiencies are found where multiple components’ weaknesses are simultaneously compensated—i.e., the greatest reservoirs of the plateau.

In practice:

· Define plateau boundaries as tolerance bands around the trust floor, VSWR baseline, and Fano pass criteria.
· Log when the system is on a plateau (CYAN) versus off-plateau (AMBER/RED).
· Use the VNA buddy to map the plateau’s edges: as VSWR drifts, the plateau shrinks; when calibration is fresh, the plateau expands.

---

4. “Plateau Ocean Front, Conceptual Arizona”

This is the name for the conceptual community formed by the components and their buddy relations. It is not a physical location; it is a design metaphor:

· Ocean Front: the boundary where the system meets the real RF environment—dynamic, noisy, and always changing. The VNA sits at this front.
· Conceptual Arizona: the internal, stable, model-driven region—the simulation-first kernel, the provenance hypergraph, the dual quadration logic. It is “conceptual” because it is not yet validated against the physical world.
· Plateau: the high-efficiency region where the Ocean Front and Conceptual Arizona overlap—where real RF data and internal model agree within tolerance.

The teleprompter’s job is to show the operator where the system is on this map: on a plateau (CYAN), approaching the edge (AMBER), or off the plateau (RED).

---

5. Revised VNA Integration (Buddy Model)

1. VNA → Trust Score: VNA variance contributes a buddy weight to the trust score, but does not override Fano or quadration.
2. VNA → Fano Input: VSWR/return-loss sweep is one input to the heptadic validator; a severe mismatch is a typed failure, not a silent discard.
3. VNA → Metal Runtime: The 16×16 matrix shows the plateau map, not just faults: Cyan = on plateau, Amber = near edge, Red = off plateau.
4. Calibration as Buddy State: CALIBRATION_DUE is a weakness flag that lowers the VNA’s buddy weight; other components compensate by increasing manual inspection.
5. No Prevention Gate: The VNA never blocks a commit. It informs the community; the community decides.

---

Bottom line

Deprecating “prevent” in favor of “buddy” means the VNA becomes a contributing member of the conceptual community, not a gatekeeper. Its weaknesses are covered by other components; its strengths expand the Baron plateau. The greatest efficiencies are found in the reservoirs where multiple buddies compensate each other’s weaknesses simultaneously—the Plateau Ocean Front of Conceptual Arizona.
